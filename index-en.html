Short reading

The S8 does not look like a marginal local line: it grows more than the other suburban lines considered, has annual volumes comparable to some Italian metros, and shows very strong growth in the Meratese stations.

The most interesting point is that growth is not concentrated only in the 7-9 peak: a very large share happens during the rest of the day, suggesting a more ordinary and distributed use of the train.

Policy and institutional relevance

The political reading remains separate from the analysis: this section makes explicit the questions that the data help raise.

The research in the Regional Council records

On 7 May 2026 I presented my S8 passenger-flow analysis at a public meeting in Merate, also attended by regional councillor Gian Mario Fragomeli. On 9 May MerateOnline reported on the meeting and its findings.

On 16 July, theODG 2329, concerning a railway crossover in Olgiate Molgora, was tabled. It repeated figures on weekday passengers and the S8's position in national comparisons. On 12 October,ODG 3184, concerning improvements to the line, explicitly referenced an analysis of regional open data published on 7 May 2026, covering 2019–2025 passenger flows, S8 stations and growth in off-peak demand.

The documents do not name the study's author, but the 12 October reference matches the date, period and content of the research presented in Merate. Both orders of the day were rejected, on 23 July and 21 October 2026 respectively. Another order of the day concerning the Olgiate crossover,ODG 3190 was submitted in October and also rejected. The infrastructure proposal predates this research; no claim is made to having originated it.

Policy relevance

This analysis provides a transparent evidence base for public discussion on the S8 Milano-Lecco rail line. The data suggest substantial demand growth compared with 2019, not limited to the traditional 7-9 morning peak, and indicate that the line increasingly works as everyday territorial infrastructure for the Meratese area.

These findings raise planning questions about frequency, reliability, capacity, station accessibility and integration with buses, cycling infrastructure and parking.

Evidence-based priorities

The data indicate that the S8 should not be read only as a morning commuter corridor. Growth distributed across the day makes off-peak service, local connections, station quality and real accessibility for people living in the line's catchment area relevant planning issues.

Policy engagement

The analysis has circulated in local public discussion and has supported evidence-based conversations on rail demand, territorial access and service planning. In some cases, station-level deep dives were prepared to respond to questions raised in local conversations.

My contribution remains technical and analytical: data, methods, visualisations and interpretation. Political proposals are kept separate from the evidence base.

Limits of the analysis

This analysis does not directly measure overcrowding, suppressed demand, operational quality or the causal effect of specific timetable changes. It should inform public discussion and institutional questions, not replace operational planning or internal data held by transport providers and regional authorities.

Main definitions

Saliti24H indicates passengers boarding at the station across the day. Saliti7-9 indicates the morning peak band. Indices are normalised with 2019=100 as the base.

Why some comparisons are delicate

The data comes from ridership campaigns, not continuous counts. For this reason, 2024-2025 comparisons use only campaigns that are present in both years.
